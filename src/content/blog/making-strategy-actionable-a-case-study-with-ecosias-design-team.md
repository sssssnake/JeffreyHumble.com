“How do we get the team using our strategy?”

When teams finally get a moment to decompress, they often say things like that. Team leads might find it challenging to know where to start.

I’ve found that “being more strategic” often means: *we need to understand what matters so we can find ways to focus.*

That was the starting point for this workshop with the Ecosia design team. They wanted practical ways to get aligned on strategy.

They weren’t lacking strategy—in fact, Ecosia had recently updated its design strategy. But like many design teams, they were grappling with a familiar tension: the strategy existed, yet it didn’t feel actionable. It was hard to tell how daily work connected to the bigger mission and vision. The question wasn’t “what’s the strategy?”—it was “what do we do with it?”

That’s where I came in. As a part of the team’s offsite, I facilitated a custom workshop for the team to slow down, zoom out, and look at their work through a strategic lens.

We didn’t write a new strategy—we *workshopped* the strategy they already had. And I think it’s something every design team should do at their offsites to renew their purpose and direction.

## 3. How We Structured the Day

Three key parts of the workshop were clearly laid out:

### 1. Sensemaking

- Reviewed the existing strategy, including the updated mission.
- Asked: *What feels clear? What feels fuzzy?*
- Encouraged the team to acknowledge confusion and surface friction points.

### 2. Reframing

- Shifted the mindset: Strategy isn’t fixed—teams participate in shaping it.
- Encouraged a new posture: Stop asking “What’s the strategy?” and start asking “What bets are we placing?”
- Explored design’s role in making strategy visible and testable.

### 3. Actioning

- Moved from abstract goals to real team choices.
- Defined current focus areas using sticky notes and open prompts like:

  - “Things we’re exploring”
  - “Things we’ve already committed to”
  - “Things we might want to stop”
