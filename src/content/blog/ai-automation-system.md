If you’ve ever used Zapier, Slack, or an AI agent to manage work, this article is for you.

I hacked this automation system together to handle **notifications, knowledge management, and automation** in a central, collaborative place that **costs nothing** to run.

Why did I build such a thing? It all started a year ago. With all the hype around AI, I decided it was time to get serious about automation.

I wasn’t looking to generate images. I was looking to manage my work life better and automate tasks. After a few years of founding a company, I was drowning in a sea of tools. I knew there was a coming wave of AI automation, and I wanted to learn the basics of automating tasks.

So, I set off on my quest for a personal operating system that would unite and automate all of the notifications, connections, and bots that can take me into the age of AI….A single place where I could see everything I do as a founder, researcher, and designer.

## What I Wanted from an Automation System

![email sucks](/images/f8d653fc-258f-43b3-8c4d-18a55cad2542/Inbox_and_spam.png)

Not everybody needs this kind of system.

Most will be good with a few Zapier integrations. But if you want to get serious about automation, you need a system that can handle the full spectrum of AI tools that won’t break the bank.

Why did I need an automation system?

1. **I hate using email.** It feels like the spam never ends. Some tools can help (check out [Leave Me Alone](https://leavemealone.com/)) to fight the spam, but it’s only a matter of time before it gets out of control. Since starting the [Fountain Institute blog](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog), I’ve been inundated with cold-call sales emails and backlink requests. It feels like the spam never ends, and I wanted to take control of it.
2. **I can’t miss critical business notifications.** I run a small online school called the [Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/), and I want to be notified immediately when someone fills out a customer feedback message or books a call with me. I wanted a dashboard-like experience to see mission-critical notifications and some way to tag their status.
3. **I don’t want spammy notifications, so** I turn off all non-human notifications on my phone and computer. I do this because I want control over my attention. I don’t want some growth team notifying me just to boost their quarterly numbers. Sometimes, I miss things because of this, but I’m usually willing to take that risk to pursue a purpose-driven user experience with my tech.
4. **I needed a central place to manage multi-tool workflows.** As a bootstrapped founder, I used multiple cheap tools from different companies to deliver my service. Bootstrapped founders get used to looping tools like a guitarist with a board of guitar pedals. For example, I needed four tools to register a new customer completely, but I didn’t have an interface besides email that tracked everything.
5. **I wanted to automate work using bots and AI agents.** AI's interface is often text-based and browser-based. Some of the newest AI tools, like [Midjourney](https://www.midjourney.com/home) and [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt/), don’t talk to each other or play well with other tools (for now). I wanted something that would bring multiple AI models together in a single place.
6. **I didn’t want to pay monthly fees.** I hate subscriptions more than I hate email. I will gladly settle for a less useful tool if there is a lifetime payment option or a free, open-source alternative. As you’ll see, there almost always is an open-source option (try [Open Alternative](https://openalternative.co/self-hosted) for a nice list of free tools).
7. **I** **wanted a system that could scale to an entire product team.** I hoped this experience would help me manage research operations at organizations that need to automate product discovery. Therefore, it was essential that I could do this not just for myself but also for the entire product team.
8. **I wanted a tool for life, not just work.** Email and productivity tools are just so dull. I wanted a place to send myself a quote, a recipe, or an image I liked. I could use Notion, Evernote, or message myself, but I didn’t want a separate tool. And most tools are organized for work or pleasure, not both.

There might be some custom solution that could do all of this for the price of a Macbook Pro every month, but that’s not the kind of budget I had.

As you’ll see, it’s **possible to do it for free,** thanks to the magic of **free trials** and **open-source software,**at least for now.

## My Personal Automation System

After trying out a few tools, I ended up with something that I use every single day.

Here is a birds-eye-view of my system:

![automation tools open source free trial](/images/066f2264-f944-47f9-b714-752c7ce3bdf5/Subscription-Free_Automation-Jeff-Humble.png)

It’s a private Discord account with hyper-specific channels connected to various tools in my tech stack that Activepieces, an open-source automation platform, pulls into Discord.

The Discord is the first thing I check in the morning, and I love how I can tweak the notifications so that I only get notified from my Discord. When I hear the Discord noise on my computer, I know something important has happened. I certainly can’t say that about email or Slack because others can message me on a whim.

Here is a screenshot of the current setup in Discord where I am the only user:

At the moment, this system is just for me, but it would cost nothing to add dozens of people to this Discord server. I like that it has the ability to grow, but that I can experiment in private to learn the system.

### Channel Breakdown

Each channel is a working prototype of something I’m trying to archive or automate. It’s way easier to handle than browser tabs.

Here is a breakdown of the channels I’m experimenting with:

- **# 🤖-ai-assistants** - pulls in AI Agents from Chat GPT that I can chat with in [Discord](https://discord.com/)
- **# 💰-sales-notifications** - pulls in any sale in [Stripe](https://stripe.com)
- **# 💼-business-admin** - a feed where I put receipts & other docs I need to save (for taxes)
- **# 💼-content-to-post** - a feed where I save ideas & memes I want to post on [social media](https://bit.ly/m/jeffhumble)
- **# 💼-disco-registrations** - pulls in notifications for students registering for [my course](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/defining-ux-strategy)
- **# 💼-calendar-notifications** - pulls in notifications of people booking me on [Cal.com](https://cal.com)
- **# 💼-jeff-site-forms** - pulls in form submissions on [my personal website](/home)
- **# 💼-fountain-forms** - pulls in form submissions on the [Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/)
- **# 💼-continuous-research** - pulls in user interview appts. & data submitted in surveys
- **# 💼-ai-automations** - status updates on AI-powered automation from [Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/)
- **# 💼-tools** - a feed where I post new tools I’m interested in using
- **# 👤-life-admin** - a feed where I pull in appts. or important papers (a must if you live in 🇩🇪)
- **# 👤-content-just-because** - an image/video I want to save, often from social media
- **# 👤-feeds** - announcement channels in other Discords & [RSS feeds](https://rss.app/bots/rssfeeds-discord-bot) I want to follow (in Discord you can [mute a single channel](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/215253258-Notifications-Settings-101#:~:text=The%20last%20section%20of%20the,channels%20entirely%20by%20selecting%20mute.) if your RSS feed starts to feel spammy)
- **# 👤-reading-list** - Here, I drop links to books and articles I want to read
- **# 👤-aesthetics** - I use this one like an ad-free Pinterest board
- **# 👤-movies-shows** - I use this to drop links for content on [123movies](https://123movies9.bar/) or YouTube
- **# 👤-journal** - I use this to send thoughts and learnings to myself like a diary

Many of these channels came about because I was messaging myself a link and thought, “Maybe I should have a dedicated place for this?” The rest are inspired by different things that Activepieces can do.

### Tagging Content with Emojis to Indicate Status

Any item in any channel can be further tagged to give it a status for something with an emoji.

I mainly use emojis to tag Activepieces automations that makes it into my Discord. I use this to keep track of where customers are in their onboarding. Any emoji can become a tag to keep track of the customers’ status.

![tagging stripe sales in Discord Activepieces](/images/507582f2-1dee-4fbc-be5f-1fa1ca8f27fb/Stripe_Sale_Example.png)

Example of a sale in Stripe with emoji tags to indicate onboarding status

Above, you can see a Stripe sale for [Defining UX Strategy: LIVE](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/defining-ux-strategy), a course I teach at the Fountain Institute. Since this record is saved indefinitely in my Discord, it works as a sales record I can search through without email. Again, I hate email, and I love adding little notes about each sale that I can use to improve my marketing.

I use emojis to indicate whether or not I’ve done specific manual actions. I use a different marketing, sales, product, and accounting tool because I don’t have a dev that can connect all these tools automatically. I use these four emojis to indicate my onboarding actions on every sale.

The 🧾 indicates that I’ve created an invoice for the sale in Accountable, my accounting tool. The 💬 indicates I’ve sent the new customer a welcome message in the learning platform.

I know that I could automate more and more of this in the future. I think manual efforts and manually adding tags is far from automated, but at least I can trust that the task has been done when I see the emojis. You could use this kind of emoji-as-a-tag system for pretty much anything that Activepieces can pull into Discord.

### Why I like Discord more than Slack

While Slack is more user-friendly, the free trial in Discord is way better than Slack. Slack’s free trial only lets you see your messages from the past three months, which won’t do for archiving business info such as receipts and invoices. **Discord’s free trial gives the average user unlimited storage**. I hope Discord keeps this feature of the free trial, as it makes the comparison a no-brainer compared to the eight bucks a month per user that Slack charges for unlimited storage. You can even self-host a Discord if you need a complex, corporate use case.

Discord also **feels more adaptable** and open-sourced than Slack, including a million ways to personalize and automate the experience. In addition to the tools you love, hundreds of Discord-specific bots can be used to customize the experience if you’re willing to dig through the giant list of apps.

### Why I like Activepieces more than Zapier

**Activepieces is free** **for simple setups** with 1,000 free runs a month. And if that ever changes, you can self-host and pay nothing for unlimited runs. While Zapier has more integrations and is more user-friendly, it’s one of the most expensive providers. I have difficulty paying $20 a month when a free alternative exists that does almost everything Zapier does. Activepieces is an excellent open-sourced alternative to Zapier if you don’t mind a little fiddling to get it to work.

The interface is very similar so that Zapier fans can jump right in. Even though the tool is open-sourced, it’s still actively supported, with new integrations dropping weekly.

### Alternatives to Discord

You certainly don’t need to use Discord to make this kind of system work. If your employer already uses another tool like Slack, you might find it easier to use that instead.

Here are some alternatives to Discord:

- Slack - good if your company is already using this
- Notion - good if you want a more dashboard-like experience, long-form content, or more notifications with less automation
- AirTable - good if you want to turn this into a custom app with other no-code skills such as for a client

I’ve found that the tool you check more often is usually the better interface layer, so use the tool you’ve already got if you can.

### Alternatives to Activepieces

Activepieces is more of a learning curve than established tools like Zapier. If you need customer service or detailed guides, try some of these paid options:

- Zapier - has the most integrations
- Make - has a lot of new AI stuff
- Integromat - seems pretty standard
- PabblyConnect - has a lifetime version but seems complicated
- IFTTT - limited but a good place to start

## In-House Use Cases for an Automation System

![automation system consultant](/images/a2e26712-d4c9-4a8a-9459-ad84be880002/igor-omilaev-gVQLAbGVB6Q-unspl-moshed-01-23-03-09-09-460.jpg)

Not everyone will need their system to work like mine, but there are many reasons why an in-house employee might need an automation system.

Here are some of the use cases that I’ve seen this type of system work:

1. ### Business Automation

   This is the main use case and how I use my automation system. If it’s important, I want it to be visible. And if I can automate it without reducing quality,

   I probably wouldn’t have built a system like this if I were working in-house. When you’re a founder or a freelancer, the faster you do something, the more money you make. So, there is an incentive to automate that you don’t always see in-house. Of course, you will likely be rewarded for saving people time, and the increase in collaboration for a shared system is absolutely worth the effort.
2. ### Research Operations

   There’s never enough time to do research. An automation system can help you find the time and minimize the admin work. I’ve used a system like this to notify designers of upcoming interviews, gather research notes, and analyze them with AI tools without leaving Slack. The fact that you can bring ChatGPT into a Discord channel will make research analysis effortless.

   When you’ve got a big team of busy people, it pays to have a system to manage your continuous discovery habits. I’ve found this to be useful even with two people. You’d be amazed at how confusing scheduling can be, for instance, once you need to manage three calendars.

   Tools like [Calendly](https://calendly.com/) or [Cal.com](https://cal.com/) (my preferred choice because it’s free) are adding automation flows directly in the app, but they’ll always have fewer features than dedicated automation tools like Activepieces. Even if you’re a pro researcher, you can get stakeholders to care by making your research efforts transparent using a system like this.
3. ### Tracking Metrics & Projects

   Sharing marketing and sales stats with the whole team using automation setups is a common practice at startups. Sharing departmental data across the org helps reduce silos and gives people real-time data to get better at their jobs.

   But it doesn’t just have to be the sales team that sends notifications to the whole company. Using an automation flow, you can send messages as a developer or a designer when things go live. You can even set up a specific channel for every project so that every comment and meeting is archived chronologically. You can even invite clients to be collaborators in a project channel.

   You may get some pushback initially, but sharing data is always better for the team. There is no limit to the silos you can break when you give everyone access to company-wide data.
4. ### Team Hubs

   You can use this same setup to run a community of practice within your organization or as a side gig. If your company only uses email for communication, an automation system like this will likely blow them away. You can set up a system in Slack or Discord with lots of automation to bring a specific subset of the company closer via real-time notifications.

   You might also find that certain people are very passionate about automating things at your company. Why not start a Discord for your product group and experiment with AI tools? It will give you some interesting things to talk about at the office and build a sense of community at work.
