It’s all about finding the overlap between business wants and user needs.

## What is Design Strategy?

<p class="text-large">Design strategy is the creative application of the business strategy to user needs that strengthen a company’s competitive advantage.</p>

It’s a way to find those user needs that align with the business strategy and say no to the user needs that don’t align.

**A good design strategy is based on research and creativity.** To uncover that middle ground, you’ll need to understand the business, user, and market dynamics at play. It takes creativity to turn those human needs, desires, and pain points into opportunities that capture value for the company in a way that the competition hasn’t discovered yet.

**A great design strategy is based on collaboration.** Creating a design strategy will be one of the toughest projects of your life. you’re going to need all the help you can get. If you want to make sure your design strategy doesn’t get ignored in the Google Drive, you’re going to need buy-in from the whole design team, too.

## The 3 Levels of Strategy

![](/images/cbc2f123-51b6-4fcd-82a9-ab4bcf0cc944/3_levels_of_strategy-Jeff_Humble.png)

### Corporate Strategy

The business strategy is the most discussed, but it's just the middle level. When managing multiple business lines, you need a corporate strategy for things like mergers & acquisitions...way beyond your pay grade.

### Business Strategy

The business strategy is one of the most important guiding artifacts for a product person, but I bet you check your company’s social media more than their business strategy.

The business strategy at your company is essential to knowing if you're moving in the right direction.

> *Business strategy is the strategic initiatives a company pursues to create value for the organization and its stakeholders and gain a competitive advantage in the market. This strategy is crucial to a company's success and is needed before any goods or services are produced or delivered.*
>
> -Harvard Business School

Business strategy is all about competitive advantage in the market, meaning you create value for customers in a way nobody else can.

Align to the business strategy, and you should always be on the right side of decision-making. But what if the business strategy isn't specific enough to guide design?

It's a common problem, and it's completely normal. That's why there are three levels of strategy.

### Functional Strategy

But you want to pay attention to the functional strategy or, actually, strategies.

At the functional level, you'd find many different team strategies like the UX, product, brand, and all the others. The main goal of a functional strategy is to apply the business strategy to that functional department.

## Design Strategy as a Functional Strategy

A crucial role of product is to stay in touch with user needs and expectations. If you're doing that, you'll probably hear things from users that don't align with the business strategy.

For example, Let’s say your strategy is to monetize empty homes with a two-sided marketplace between homeowners and tourists like Airbnb. So what if users tell you they'd like to save their favorite host and message them outside the app?

There are some user desires you can't grant because they contradict your business strategy.

So you have to say no to the user needs that align with the business strategy and yes to the ones that strengthen the business strategy.

That overlap is your design strategy.

It helps you say no to the things that won't enforce the business's strengths.

## 4 contexts of design strategy
